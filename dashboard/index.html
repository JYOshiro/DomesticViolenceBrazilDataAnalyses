CAPSTONE INVESTIGATION / BRAZIL / 2012–2026

I started with alcohol. The data changed the question.

This investigation began by asking whether beer consumption and taxation could explain domestic-violence reports. Each test exposed a limitation, changed the next question, and ultimately shifted the goal from explaining why violence occurs to anticipating when recorded demand may be higher.

STARTING ASSUMPTION

Alcohol may influence domestic violence. Do beer consumption and taxation help explain the reporting pattern?

FINDING

No clear relationship appears in the two trajectories.

Beer volume is a company sales measure and the protection series counts source rows rather than unique victims. The comparison is exploratory—not a causal test.

UNEXPECTED RESULT

Female suspects and mothers appear more often than the original partner-violence framing implied.

The extract covers broad family and domestic violence, including violence involving children, older people, parents, carers, and other relatives. It is not limited to male-partner violence against women.

Change in thinking The outcome is broader than the starting assumption.

REPORTING RECORDS

Annual source rows with a 2020–21 comparability break

The visible jump occurs in 2021, after the initial 2020 observation. Later totals continue at a different scale.

DISCOVERY

Changes in reporting and classification can change the count without an equivalent change in underlying violence.

The database does not include a stable report identifier or a validated Disque 100/Ligue 180 program split. Pre- and post-break values are therefore shown as reporting context, not a continuous incidence series.
